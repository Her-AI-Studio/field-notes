The night was warm and humid, with a barometric pressure of 29.92 inHg and a waxing crescent moon providing minimal light. Conditions were ideal for nocturnal insect activity.

## Method

I set up a UV light trap at 20:30, approximately 50 meters from the nearest trail, in a mixed cove hardwood forest dominated by tulip poplar (*Liriodendron tulipifera*) and eastern hemlock (*Tsuga canadensis*). The sheet was positioned facing south, with a slight clearing to maximize visibility.

## Collection Log

### Lepidoptera

The moth activity was exceptional, likely due to the recent rains and high humidity.

1. **Luna moth** (*Actias luna*) — 1 male. Arrived at 21:15. Wingspan approximately 10 cm. The pale green coloration was striking against the white sheet. It remained for nearly an hour before disappearing into the canopy.

2. **Rosy maple moth** (*Dryocampa rubicunda*) — 3 individuals. These small, pink-and-yellow moths arrived in quick succession around 21:30. They were skittish and difficult to photograph.

3. **Underwing moth** (*Catocala sp.*) — 2 individuals. Likely *Catocala ultronia*. The bright red hindwings were only visible in flight; at rest, the cryptically patterned forewings made them nearly invisible against tree bark.

4. **Imperial moth** (*Eacles imperialis*) — 1 female. A magnificent specimen, arriving at 22:40. The yellow and purple-brown patterning was particularly vivid.

### Coleoptera

Beetle activity was moderate, with several interesting species:

- **Eastern eyed click beetle** (*Alaus oculatus*) — 1 specimen. The large false eyespots on the pronotum are remarkably convincing.
- **Firefly** (*Photinus pyralis*) — Numerous. Observed flashing patterns but did not collect.
- **Bess beetle** (*Odontotaenius disjunctus*) — 2 specimens. Found crawling on the sheet rather than flying to it.

## Observations

> The diversity of moths in this single location, on a single night, is a powerful reminder of the unseen abundance that surrounds us. We walk through a world teeming with life that only reveals itself after dark.

I noted that the peak activity period was between 21:30 and 23:00, with numbers declining sharply after midnight. Temperature dropped from 24°C at 20:30 to 19°C by 01:00, which likely contributed to the decline.

## Specimens Collected

- *Actias luna* — photographed and released
- *Dryocampa rubicunda* — 1 specimen collected for reference
- *Eacles imperialis* — photographed and released
- *Alaus oculatus* — collected

## Notes for Next Survey

- Bring a larger light source — the 15W UV bulb was adequate but a 40W would likely attract more specimens
- Set up earlier in the evening (dusk is when many species become active)
- Bring better macro photography equipment
- Consider using a bait trap in addition to the light trap

Survey concluded at 01:30. Total species observed: 14 (estimated).
